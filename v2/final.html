How this page came together

The lecture page you just read (or skimmed) wasn't written in one pass. It was produced by an agentic loop: the same AI wrote each draft, then a second, completely separate AI session — with no memory of previous rounds, no access to prior scores, and its own real browser to render the page in — graded it cold. This tab is the log of that loop.

The process, briefly

Each round, a "Builder" session wrote or revised the page in direct response to the previous round's feedback. Then an isolated "Critic" subagent — seeing only the file path, never the Builder's reasoning, the round number, or any prior critic report — scored it on two axes out of 10: Content (does it cover what a loop is, when to use one, and its pros and cons, accurately and at the right pace?) and Design (does it pass a fixed mechanical checklist — palette, contrast, spacing, type — and then hold up against five reference images once actually rendered in a browser?). The loop would stop as soon as both scores hit a genuine 10 in the same round, or after 8 rounds, whichever came first.

What changed, round by round

Round 1 — Content 9/10, Design 8/10
First draft, built from scratch against the design system and reference images. All four required beats were covered and the page read at roughly the right pace, but the hero and takeaway watermark icons were missing a CSS rule that keeps them as thin outlines — without it, they rendered as solid black shapes. Nothing in the source code looked wrong; only the actual screenshot caught it.
Round 1 (9/8) → Round 2 (10/9)
The watermark bug was fixed, and about 450 words were added across the page to close a pacing gap (Round 1 read in roughly 7 minutes against a 10-minute target). Content reached a perfect 10. The rendered check then caught something new: the card lists in two sections were packed into a cramped 2-column grid, noticeably tighter than the reference image's single spacious column.
Round 2 (10/9) → Round 3 (10/9)
Those card lists were rebuilt as a single stacked column with larger badges and more padding, matching the reference. The rendered check moved on to a new target: two back-to-back paragraphs in Section 1 read denser and more textbook-like than anything in the five reference images.
Round 3 (10/9) → Round 4 (10/9)
Section 1 was restructured to remove the dense paragraph stack. The rendered check then flagged the flow diagram's connector arrows: they rendered muted gray instead of the reference's crimson, which weakened the diagram's sense of direction.
Round 4 (10/9) → Round 5 (9/9)
The diagram arrows were recolored crimson to match the reference. Content actually dropped a point this round — not because the page got worse, but because Round 5's Critic, grading cold with no memory of Round 4's perfect content score, independently judged the page's "about 10 minutes" claim as an inferred word-count estimate rather than a measured read, and docked pacing for it. That's the isolation working as designed, even when it produces a score that moves backward.
Round 5 (9/9) → Round 6 (10/9)
Section 1's remaining dense note was split into two lighter, side-by-side cards. Content recovered to 10/10. The rendered check surfaced a variant of an issue first seen back in Round 2: the card-list badges rendered mostly black (`--ink`), with crimson reserved for only one "key" badge per section — more monochrome than the reference's consistently crimson badges.
Round 6 (10/9) → Round 7 (10/9)
A thin crimson ring was added to the default badge as a middle ground — a border, not a solid fill, to stay inside the design system's crimson-dominance limit. The rendered check then noticed every section-head marker icon reused the exact same glyph regardless of topic, and that both watermark icons rendered too faint next to the reference's bolder treatment.
Round 7 (10/9) → Round 8 (10/9, final)
The five section markers were given distinct, topic-relevant icons, and both watermarks were thickened and enlarged. All three of Round 7's fixes verified as resolved. The rendered check found one more instance of the same underlying tension seen in Rounds 2 and 6: the default marker circle still fills ink, not crimson, while every reference image fills its icon-marker circle crimson by default. Round 8 was the 8th round, so the loop stopped here regardless of score.

How it ended

Stop reason: round cap, not a perfect score. The loop was allowed to run up to 8 rounds no matter what, and it used all 8 — Content and Design never hit a genuine 10/10 in the same round. Design plateaued at 9/10 for seven rounds straight (Round 2 onward), but not because the same bug kept recurring: each round's rendered check found a genuinely new, specific problem (a bug, then a layout gap, a color choice, a density issue, a repeated icon). Read together, though, four of those findings — Rounds 2, 6, 7, and 8 — are different faces of one unresolved tension: the reference images fill every icon badge solid crimson, while the design system's own crimson-dominance rule caps how much of the page can be crimson-filled. No single round was positioned to notice that pattern, because the Critic is deliberately isolated and cold every round — it only shows up when you read all eight reports side by side, which is what this page's `run-summary.md` does explicitly.
